Notebook 20: The Delayed-Choice Quantum Eraser as an Indivisible Process¶

The question. In Kim et al.'s eraser the idler holds a perfect record of the path at t1 and never interacts with the signal photon again, so by Paper 1 §3.7 t1 is a division event for the signal photon. The pair does not divide then, consistent with Barandes: the record sits inside the pair, and Paper 1 §3.9 (eq. 65) has a composite divide through "interactions between one of the subsystems and the larger environment". The sharp case is later, at t2: detector D0, a genuine outside environment, records the signal's position x, but x does not fix the idler's path. If the pair divided at t2, the fringes Kim et al. measure in the D1-sorted data would vanish (§6: visibility 1 → 0). So does eq. 65 require the environment's record to fix the composite's configuration, making a subsystem's record enough only when the partner's configuration is a function of it?

Goal: Put Kim et al.'s delayed-choice quantum eraser on the same footing as the double slit of notebook 05, as one stochastic process whose configurations are photon positions and detector clicks. Then ask what indivisibility says about "erasure", about the delay, and about division events. This is a translation and a new application of ISP, not new physics; the prior work is credited section by section.

By the end of this notebook you will be able to:

  • Reproduce Kim et al.'s four joint-detection patterns R01–R04 from one joint table P(x, d)
  • Read "sorting by the idler detector" as conditioning, and measure each sorted sub-ensemble's divisibility defect
  • See that the unsorted screen composes through the path while two of the four sorted sub-ensembles do not
  • State the predictability–visibility relation detector by detector (Englert & Bergou), as a trade-off between path record and indivisibility
  • Show that Barandes's division-event criterion (Paper 1 §3.7) says t1 divides photon 1, while a persistent-record test on the pair says t1 does not divide the pair
  • Answer the run-by-run version of the division-event question
  • Show why the 8 ns delay does no work, and what the eraser says about objective division events

Sources. "Paper 1, 2, 3" are Jacob A. Barandes's papers:

  • Paper 1: "The Stochastic-Quantum Correspondence", arXiv:2302.10778 (Philosophy of Physics 3(1), 8, 2025).
  • Paper 2: "The Stochastic-Quantum Theorem", arXiv:2309.03085.
  • Paper 3: "Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes", arXiv:2507.21192 (Stud. Hist. Phil. Sci. 119, 102213, 2026).

The experiment: Y.-H. Kim, R. Yu, S. P. Kulik, Y. H. Shih and M. O. Scully, "A Delayed Choice Quantum Eraser", arXiv:quant-ph/9903047, Phys. Rev. Lett. 84, 1 (2000), after Scully and Drühl, Phys. 1. The Experiment as Configurations¶

A pump beam hits a crystal in two regions, A and B (the "slits"). A pair of photons is born in one of them. The signal photon goes a short way to detector D0, which scans the far-field pattern. The idler goes a long way (about 2.5 m more, so at least 8 ns later) through a maze of beamsplitters:

  • transmitted at BS_A or BS_B → D3 (came from A) or D4 (came from B): which-path detectors;
  • reflected → a final 50/50 beamsplitter BS → D1 or D2: the path is erased.

The configurations, in time order:

t0  source
t1  path        signal and idler both in A, or both in B
t2  signal at D0 pixel x                     (early)
t3  idler at D1, D2, D3 or D4                (late)

On Barandes's reading (Paper 1, §3.5), the pair really is born in A or in B on every run, and the signal really lands at one pixel. The model's only inputs are the signal arm (the far-field DFT of notebook 05) and a 4×2 idler isometry V (see isp/eraser.py). The joint table is P(x, d) = |ψ(x, d)|², with ψ(x, d) = (1/√2) Σ_s U2[x, s] V[d, s].

2. Kim et al.'s Four Patterns, and the Flat Screen¶

Kim et al. report R01 and R02 with fringes shifted by π, and R03, R04 without fringes (their Figs. 3–5). The D0 singles, which never look at the idler, are predicted to show no fringes at all (Kim et al. do not report them separately; R01 + R02 has no fringe).

Model limitation: the slits here are points on a pixel grid, so there is no single-slit (sinc²) envelope. The D0 singles and R03/R04 come out flat, whereas Kim et al.'s data sit under the sinc² envelope of the finite slit width (their expressions for R01 and R02). Only the fringe structure is modelled.

3. Sorting Is Conditioning: the Divisibility Defect of Each Sub-Ensemble¶

Notebook 05 showed that the fringe is the divisibility defect through the slit plane (Barandes's eq. 43, Paper 1 §3.5). Do the same here, once per idler detector:

Δ(x | d) = P(x | d) − Σ_s P(x | s) P(s | d)

Δ = 0 means the d-sorted pattern is what you get by fixing the path at t1 and propagating it, a mixture of the two single-path patterns. Δ ≠ 0 means it is not.

Reading.

  • The D3/D4 sub-ensembles compose through the path: their idler click is a path record, and conditioning on it conditions on the configuration at t1.
  • The D1/D2 sub-ensembles do not: each is a fringe, and the fringe is its defect, exactly as in notebook 05.
  • Averaged over detectors the defects cancel, so the unsorted screen composes through the path. Whether t1 is a division event depends on whose process is asked about, photon 1's or the pair's; Section 4 compares the two.
  • Nothing about photon 1 changes when the idler clicks. The four patterns are four sub-tables of one table. Sorting picks out sub-ensembles of runs. It does not change the past.

Prior work: the qualitative story (definite path, sorted fringes, no retrocausality) is not new. Catani, Leifer, Schmid & Spekkens (Quantum 7, 1119, 2023; arXiv:2111.13727) reproduce the eraser in a classical, epistemically restricted toy theory; Griffiths (quant-ph/9810016) and Hiley & Callaghan (arXiv:1602.06100) give no-retrocausality readings of delayed choice. The sorted defect itself is Barandes's eq. 43 cross term inside one sub-ensemble, i.e. Englert & Bergou's sorted interference term (Section 5); calling it a divisibility defect is a relabelling.

4. Was There a Division Event at t1?¶

The entanglement at t1 writes the path into the idler. For the D3/D4 runs that record survives; for the D1/D2 runs it is erased. To ask whether the pair divides at t1 we use a working test from an earlier lab study, the persistent-record test: t1 is a division event of the joint process to accuracy ε only if the persistent-record bound stays below ε at every later time:

bound(t) = 2 Σ_z |b(z, A)| |b(z, B)|

where b(z, s) is the history amplitude from the source through path s at t1 to the joint configuration z at time t. The actual defect, Σ_z |P(z) − P_divided(z)|, is what you lose by restarting the process from the path at t1. Caveat: the bound needs the joint transitions re-rooted at t1, which Γ(· ← 0) alone does not supply, so it is not a Γ-only criterion; this model supplies them. Its second step is the triangle inequality for history vectors. It is used here as a working definition, not as a result.

The bound is an Englert–Bergou quantity. Englert & Bergou (Opt. Commun. 179, 337 (2000)) sort the photons by the outcome k of a measurement on the path's "environment" (here: the idler detector d) and define the quantum-erasure visibility V^QE = Σ_k p_k V_k, the outcome-weighted visibility of the sorted fringes (their eq. 30). From positivity they bound it by 2 Σ_k a_k b_k with a_k b_k = √(P(k, A) P(k, B)) (their eqs. 75–76). The bound above is that right-hand side, with the screen position x counted as part of the record: for a far-field screen (equal moduli over x, as here) the two coincide; in general ours is ≤ theirs by Cauchy–Schwarz. For a pure record it also equals V^QE itself, which the code below checks.

Results.

  • The pair does not divide at t1 on Kim's setup. The bound is 0 at t2 and r|sin 2θ| = 0.5 at t3, and the actual defect is 0.30. By the persistent-record test, applied to the joint configuration of both photons, t1 is not a division event for the pair. The test fires only on the edges of the plot (r = 0: no eraser; θ = 0 or π/2: the "eraser" is another which-path measurement).
  • Barandes's own criterion says t1 is a division event for photon 1. Paper 1 §3.7 (eqs. 45–56) derives a division event for a subject system from two conditions: at t′ its environment holds a perfect record of the system's configuration, and afterwards the two evolve without interacting. Take photon 1 as the subject and the idler as its environment: both conditions hold at t1, and photon 1's own Γ divides there (signal-marginal defect 0 at t2 and t3, printed above).
  • So photon 1 divides at t1 and the pair does not, and the erasure lives in the gap. Sorting photon 1 by the idler's later click is a statement about the joint process, which did not divide at t1. This agrees with Paper 1 §3.9 (eqs. 63–66), where a composite divides at a later time "perhaps due to interactions between one of the subsystems and the larger environment, as spelled out in Subsection 3.7" (just before eq. 65): at t1 the only record is the idler, inside the pair, so nothing outside the pair has recorded anything. The open case is t2 (Section 6).
  • The two defects are one defect. The joint defect at t3 is exactly the detector-weighted sum of the sorted defects of Section 3, so "erasure brings the fringes back" and "t1 was not a division event for the pair" are the same number.
  • The test's verdict on t1 is settled only after t2. At t2 the bound is 0: everything that has happened so far is consistent with a division at t1. Whether t1 was a division event of the pair is fixed by what the idler meets later. If θ is chosen after D0 fires (delayed choice proper, as in Ma et al., PNAS 110, 1221 (2013), arXiv:1206.6578), the verdict on t1 depends on a later choice. No configuration and no statistic of photon 1 changes, so this is not retrocausation; but it means that under this test "division event of the pair" is not a fact local in time. It is also why a causal reset of the pair at the first perfect record is falsified by erasers (Section 7) while the retrospective test agrees with quantum mechanics.

Prior work. The number r|sin 2θ| is Englert & Bergou's quantum-erasure visibility of Kim's sorting (see above); for this far-field model the persistent-record bound equals the right-hand side of their eq. (76). What is ours is reading it as the ISP division-event test for t1. The divisibility structure of interferometers is studied in two papers that are closely related, the same physics in different language, though neither treats the quantum eraser (beyond a passing mention):

  • Siltanen, Kuusela & Piilo, "Interferometric Approach to Open Quantum Systems and Non-Markovian Dynamics" (arXiv:2008.02826): path-wise dephasing inside an interferometer is Markovian "while the joint dynamics displays non-Markovian features", and measuring the path can create or destroy memory effects. This is the open-systems counterpart of this section's "marginal composes, joint process not divided".
  • Kam & Wong, "Conditioning on the Future: A Filtration-Theoretic Formalization of Wheeler's Participatory Universe" (arXiv:2609.19025): classical conditioning (Doob h-transforms, Markov bridges) for Wheeler's delayed choice and a Mach–Zehnder with and without a which-path monitor; they cite Barandes but call their aim orthogonal to his.

What we did not find in these papers, in Englert & Bergou, or in two literature passes is a published ISP treatment of the quantum eraser: Barandes's §3.7 criterion and a persistent-record test on the pair applied to Kim et al.'s setup and found to disagree, with the pair's verdict on t1 settled only after t2. The qualitative idea that erasing the record undoes the division event does circulate informally in online discussion of Barandes's framework (not peer-reviewed). This is a new application of ISP, not new physics.

4b. Run by run: is t1 a division event on the D3 runs?¶

Restrict the persistent-record test to the runs that end at one idler detector d: sum the bound and the defect only over later configurations z = (x, d) with that d, and divide by P(d).

Results.

  • Restricted to the D3/D4 runs, the bound and defect are exactly 0. On the D1/D2 runs the bound per run is |sin 2θ| (1 for Kim's setup) and the defect per run is the sorted defect of Section 3.
  • The whole-ensemble bound is the P(d)-weighted average of the per-run bounds. In post-selected terms, Kim's 0.5 is "half the runs look divided at t1, half maximally not".
  • So the run-by-run question has a clean answer, but it is a post-selected answer: which kind of run this is becomes known only at t3. A reset theory that divides at t1 exactly on the runs that will end at D3/D4 predicts the same table (dividing those runs changes nothing, since their defect is 0), so it is empirically equivalent and as future-dependent as the test itself.
  • Barandes defines division events for the process, not for sub-ensembles picked out by later outcomes, so "t1 was a division event on the D3/D4 runs" is not an ISP statement. It is consistent-histories language: the per-run statement is the counterpart of consistent-histories reasoning (the path family is consistent together with the D3/D4 outcomes and not with D1/D2; Griffiths, quant-ph/9810016, treats Wheeler's delayed choice this way). Likely known in substance in that language; not lit-checked for the eraser.

5. Predictability and Visibility, Detector by Detector¶

Replace the 50/50 eraser with a beamsplitter at angle θ (θ = π/4 is Kim's; θ = 0 makes D1 and D2 which-path too). For each detector d, the path predictability 𝒫_d = |P(A | d) − P(B | d)| of the d-sorted sub-ensemble and the visibility V_d of its fringe satisfy 𝒫_d² + V_d² ≤ 1 (Englert & Bergou 2000, eq. 78), with equality for a pure record (their eqs. 7 and 11 applied to the sub-ensemble). The unsorted form P² + V² ≤ 1 (their eq. 36) is due to Greenberger & Yasin and to Jaeger, Shimony & Vaidman, with an entropic precursor in Wootters & Zurek.

Terminology, following Englert & Bergou: 𝒫_d is a predictability of one sub-ensemble, not Englert's distinguishability D, which is the best which-path knowledge over all ways of reading the idler (their eq. 26); Englert's duality V² + D² ≤ 1 (PRL 77, 2154 (1996)) concerns the unsorted visibility. Kim's own sorting has which-path knowledge K = Σ_d P(d) 𝒫_d = (1 − r) + r|cos 2θ| and erasure visibility V^QE = r|sin 2θ| (Section 4); K² + (V^QE)² = 0.5 for Kim, so it is neither a which-path sorting nor an erasure sorting but half of each.

Pure records. The d-sorted defect is the cross term of the two path amplitudes (Barandes's eq. 43, one sub-ensemble at a time). Its single Fourier amplitude is V_d/2; the L1 ratio printed above is a fixed property of the fringe shape on this pixel grid (2/π ≈ 0.637 in the continuum; 0.6036 for this slit pair, the only one computed here). So "𝒫_d² + V_d² = 1" reads as "path record² + (scaled defect)² = 1": a restatement.

Caveat on P(s | d). The comparison Σ_s P(x | s) P(s | d) uses the joint probability of the path at t1 and the detector at t3. ISP assigns joint probabilities only between the conditioning time 0 and one later time; joint probabilities at two later times are left unspecified (Paper 1 §2.2). So P(s | d) is the would-be division's prediction, the right comparison for eq. 43, not a claim about what happened at t1 on the D1 runs.

5b. Imperfect records¶

Now let the path leave a marker |m_s⟩ in the idler that need not be orthogonal (|⟨m_A|m_B⟩| > 0), plus a copy |e_s⟩ in an environment nobody measures, with unbalanced arms (P(A) ≠ 1/2), and read the idler in any basis W (marker_model in isp/eraser.py).

from isp.eraser import marker_model, fringe_visibility_exact

rng = np.random.default_rng(5)
def pair(ov):
    Q, _ = np.linalg.qr(rng.normal(size=(2, 2)) + 1j * rng.normal(size=(2, 2)))
    return Q @ np.array([1, 0], complex), Q @ np.array([ov, np.sqrt(1 - abs(ov) ** 2)], complex)
def haar2():
    Q, R = np.linalg.qr(rng.normal(size=(2, 2)) + 1j * rng.normal(size=(2, 2)))

    Psd = mm['P_s_given_d'][:, okd]
    Dd = np.abs(Psd[0] - Psd[1])
    Vd = fringe_visibility_exact(mm['P_x_given_d'][:, okd])
    gap = 1 - Dd**2 - Vd**2
    worst_identity = max(worst_identity, np.abs(gap - 4 * Psd[0] * Psd[1] * (1 - mm['kappa'][okd]**2)).max())
    Delta = mm['P_x_given_d'][:, okd] - mm['P_x_given_s'] @ Psd
    Pxs = mm['P_x_given_s']
    bnd = 2 * np.sqrt(np.outer(Pxs[:, 0] * Pxs[:, 1], Psd[0] * Psd[1]))
    worst_bound = max(worst_bound, (np.abs(Delta) / (bnd + 1e-300)).max())
    S.extend(Dd**2 + Vd**2)

print('500 random imperfect records, every detector that fires:')
print(f'  max Pred_d^2 + V_d^2                                = {max(S):.5f}  (<= 1)')
print(f'  max |gap - 4 P(A|d) P(B|d) (1 - kappa_d^2)|         = {worst_identity:.1e}')
print(f'  max |Delta(x|d)| / 2 sqrt(P(x|A)P(x|B)P(A|d)P(B|d)) = {worst_bound:.5f}  (<= 1)')

500 random imperfect records, every detector that fires:
  max Pred_d^2 + V_d^2                                = 1.00000  (<= 1)
  max |gap - 4 P(A|d) P(B|d) (1 - kappa_d^2)|         = 4.4e-15
  max |Delta(x|d)| / 2 sqrt(P(x|A)P(x|B)P(A|d)P(B|d)) = 0.99393  (<= 1)

What this shows.

  • Per detector, 𝒫_d² + V_d² ≤ 1, and the gap is exactly 4 P(A|d) P(B|d) (1 − κ_d²), where κ_d is the fraction of coherence that sorting on d leaves (1 for a pure record). The missing coherence went to the environment. This is Englert & Bergou's eqs. (7) and (11) applied to the d-sorted sub-ensemble, with κ_d their |ε_k|.
  • A bound on the sorted defect. |Δ(x | d)| ≤ 2 √(P(x|A) P(x|B) P(A|d) P(B|d)). This is the x-resolved form of Englert & Bergou's positivity bound on the cross term (their eqs. 19 and 75), and nothing in it is specific to ISP. It is not a Γ-level statement: P(x | s) and P(A | d) are two-time quantities that Γ(· ← 0) does not supply (see the caveat on P(s | d) above).

5c. What the realised Γ cannot see¶

In [9]:

Two records give identical tables when the idler is read for the path, yet one can be fully erased and the other not at all. Which-path tables come from the main diagonal of the path–record state and fringes from its off-diagonal, so the first cannot fix the second (Englert & Bergou, Fig. 2; their eqs. 48–50 are the "locked" case: full which-path knowledge, coherence C = 0). Erasability is a property of the alternative laws (other settings of W), not of the realised process. This matters for Section 4: the persistent-record test inspects only the realised process, so "was t1 a division event?" depends on which law was actually run, not on a counterfactual one.

Prior work (this section is a translation). Englert & Bergou, "Quantitative quantum erasure", Opt. Commun. 179, 337 (2000), read in the original: sorting by an environment measurement, predictability P, which-path knowledge K, distinguishability D, quantum-erasure visibility V^QE and coherence C; P ≤ K ≤ D, V ≤ V^QE ≤ C; the erasure relation P² + C² ≤ 1; K² + (V^QE)² ≤ 1 for every sorting; and 𝒫_k² + V_k² ≤ 1 per sub-ensemble. Before them: Wootters & Zurek, Phys. Rev. D 19, 473 (1979); Greenberger & Yasin, Phys. Lett. A 128, 391 (1988); Jaeger, Shimony & Vaidman, PRA 51, 54 (1995); Englert, PRL 77, 2154 (1996); Björk & Karlsson, PRA 58, 3477 (1998), who introduced K ("measured distinguishability") and V^QE ("conditioned visibility"). Experiment: Dürr & Rempe, Opt. Commun. 179, 323 (2000), tested K² + (V^QE)² ≤ 1 and its per-sub-ensemble form. Later: Schwindt, Kwiat & Englert, PRA 60, 4285 (1999), including "non-erasing" erasure with fully mixed markers; Jakob & Bergou, PRA 66, 062107 (2002), quant-ph/0206200 (a conditional eraser that violates the unconditional erasure relation), and quant-ph/0302075 (which ties predictability and visibility to the concurrence); Coles, Kaniewski & Wehner, Nat. Commun. 5, 5814 (2014). The bound on the sorted defect is also the Cauchy–Schwarz inequality |D(α, β)|² ≤ D(α, α) D(β, β) for a positive decoherence functional in Gell-Mann and Hartle's consistent histories (stated, e.g., in arXiv:quant-ph/9704031). Catani, Leifer, Scala, Schmid & Spekkens (arXiv:2211.09850, PRA 108, 022207 (2023)) show the quantitative duality cannot be reproduced by any noncontextual model, so this is the part of the eraser a classical toy model cannot reach.

6. The Delay Does No Work¶

In Kim et al. the signal reaches D0 at least 8 ns before the idler reaches its detectors. Swap the order (idler first) and apply the persistent-record test at the first detection, t2, two ways:

  • divide on the detected photon only (pixel x if D0 fires first, detector d if the idler fires first);
  • divide on the full joint configuration (the detected photon and the other photon's path).

Results.

  • The joint table is identical in both orders. This holds by construction: both orders build the same amplitude (the two detections act on different factors and commute), so the printed difference checks the code, not the physics.
  • Dividing on the detected photon only gives bound 0, also by construction: the detected photon's own configuration is part of every later configuration, so each later configuration has a single class.
  • The non-trivial number is the other one. Dividing the full joint configuration at t2 has bound 0.5, and doing it would erase R01's fringes (visibility 1 → 0). The other photon's path stays undivided at the first detection.
  • So after D0 fires, the correlation between x and the idler's later click is carried by the joint law still rooted at t0, the source, not by anything running from t3 back to t2. Which photon is detected first changes with the order; no prediction does.
  • This is the open case for eq. 65. At t2 the signal photon interacts with D0, an environment outside the pair, exactly the situation Paper 1 §3.9 describes ("interactions between one of the subsystems and the larger environment"). Yet the pair must not divide at t2, or R01's fringes vanish. On our reading, §3.7's derivation needs the environment to record the configuration of whatever divides; D0 records x, which fixes the signal's configuration but not the idler's path. If that reading is right, eq. 65 applies only when the record fixes the composite's configuration, i.e. when the partner's configuration is a function of the recorded one (as for perfectly correlated pairs), and an eraser is the case where it is not. Whether that is Barandes's intent is the question this notebook puts to him.
  • What remains time-asymmetric is only the test's verdict on t1 (Section 4): it is settled when the idler meets BS_A/BS_B and BS, whichever photon is detected first.

Prior work: that the order of detections cannot matter is standard (the two photons' detections commute; see e.g. Fankhauser, arXiv:1707.07884, and Catani et al. 2023, §4.3); the ISP division-event reading is the new application. A delayed choice of θ made after D0 fires (Ma et al. 2013, above) would change nothing here, because θ enters only through V.

7. A Testbed for Objective Division Events¶

Suppose division events were physical resets of the joint process rather than approximations. An earlier lab study compared four reset rules; the eraser tests them directly:

  • first-record reset: reset as soon as one perfect record exists;
  • R*-fragment reset: reset when at least R* separate fragments hold the record;
  • graded reset: coherence factor exp(−λ R τ) for R fragments held for a time τ (GRW-like);
  • retrospective: the persistent-record test of Section 4.

In Kim's setup exactly one fragment (the idler) holds a perfect path record, from t1 until the idler's paths meet at BS_A/BS_B and BS, a time τ. Kim et al.'s 2.5 m is L_i − L_0, measured to the detectors, while the record lasts only until BS. Assuming the stretch from BS to the idler detectors is no longer than L_0 (Kim et al. do not give it), τ ≥ (2.5 m)/c ≈ 8.3 ns; the λ bounds below inherit that assumption.

Results.

  • The first-record reset is falsified by Kim et al.'s own data. It resets at t1, which removes R01's and R02's fringes; Kim et al.'s Figs. 3–4 show them.
  • The R-fragment reset with R = 1 is falsified the same way; with R* ≥ 2 it is untouched: a single-photon eraser never puts the record in more than one fragment.
  • The retrospective test agrees with quantum mechanics exactly (Section 4: it never fires here; the code computes its bound rather than assuming it).
  • Barandes's §3.7 division event for photon 1, read as a physical reset of the pair, would be the first-record reset and is falsified the same way. Read as a statement about photon 1's own statistics, it agrees with the data.
  • The graded reset is barely constrained. Kim et al. report no visibility number; they attribute the reduction they see to detector size and pump divergence. Attributing all of it to the graded reset, any R01 visibility V ≥ 0.5 gives λ ≤ about 8 × 10⁷ s⁻¹ per fragment. The earlier study's other side of the window, for macroscopic records to divide fast, is λ ≫ 10⁻¹⁴ s⁻¹ (a rough estimate), so a single-photon eraser leaves over 20 orders of magnitude open. Many-body echo experiments, which reverse dynamics after records spread to many degrees of freedom, are likely sharper tests; not checked here.
  • And the graded reset is degenerate with ordinary record decoherence. Englert & Bergou (eqs. 60–62) show that ordinary dissipation of the which-path marker makes its coherence, and so the recoverable visibility, decay exponentially in time. A graded reset of the record leaves the same signature, so an eraser's visibility can bound λ but cannot tell the graded reset apart from the idler's own decoherence (or from the detector-size and pump-divergence losses Kim et al. cite).

Prior work: eraser data have been used to constrain a collapse model. R. V. Li (arXiv:2603.15628) fits Kim et al.'s data to bound the collapse constant Σ of his "chaining" model. We found no paper that uses an eraser to constrain a record-count or graded reset of the kind above; these reset rules are the lab's own working models, not published.

Summary¶

  • One joint table P(x, d) reproduces all four of Kim et al.'s patterns and the flat D0 singles.
  • The which-path sub-ensembles compose through the path; the erased sub-ensembles do not, and their fringe is their divisibility defect. Averaged over the idler, the defects cancel.
  • Barandes's division-event criterion (Paper 1 §3.7) says t1 is a division event for photon 1: the idler holds a perfect record and the photons never interact again. A persistent-record test on the pair says the pair does not divide (bound r|sin 2θ| = 0.5, which is Englert & Bergou's quantum-erasure visibility of Kim's sorting). The erasure lives in the gap. Photon 1's own statistics compose through the path at every time. Post-selected on the idler's click, the bound is 0 on the D3/D4 runs and |sin 2θ| on the D1/D2 runs (consistent-histories language, not an ISP fact); the whole-ensemble bound is the average. Which runs are which is settled only at t3.
  • Predictability and visibility trade off detector by detector (𝒫_d² + V_d² ≤ 1, equality for pure records). For imperfect records the gap is 4P(A|d)P(B|d)(1 − κ_d²), and the sorted defect obeys Englert & Bergou's positivity bound. Two records can share every which-path table yet differ in erasability. All of this translates Englert & Bergou (2000).
  • The delay does no work: the joint table is the same in either order (by construction). Dividing the pair at the first detection, t2, would destroy R01's fringes, although D0 is an outside environment interacting with one subsystem, the situation of eq. 65.
  • As a testbed for objective division events: Kim et al.'s data falsify a reset of the pair at the first perfect record (and so §3.7's division event of photon 1, if read as a reset of the pair), agree with the retrospective test, and (assuming τ ≥ 8.3 ns) bound a graded reset only weakly (λ ≲ 10⁸ s⁻¹ per fragment), without separating it from ordinary record decoherence.
  • Question for Barandes: at t2 the signal photon interacts with an outside environment (D0), as in eq. 65, yet the pair must not divide. Does eq. 65 require the environment's record to fix the composite's configuration, so that a subsystem's record suffices only when the partner's configuration is a function of it?
