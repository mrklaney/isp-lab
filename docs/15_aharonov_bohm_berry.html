Notebook 15: Aharonov–Bohm and Berry Phase, or Loop Phases in Γ¶

Goal: Γ is built from $|U_{ij}|^2$, so it throws phases away. Yet the Aharonov–Bohm effect and Berry's phase are phases with measurable consequences. Find which phases Γ keeps, how it sees them, and whether they survive division events.

By the end of this notebook you will be able to:

  • Separate phases that are gauge (Barandes's Schur–Hadamard gauge, of which lattice electromagnetic gauge is an immediate special case) from the one that is not: the flux around a loop
  • Read the Aharonov–Bohm effect off one entry of Γ on a ring: $\cos^2(\Phi/2)$ at the opposite site
  • Show that Γ can tell the sign of the flux only on rings with an odd number of sites
  • Show that records erase the flux, gradually as records get stronger
  • Recover Berry's phase (the solid angle) from Γ in the configuration basis, show that Γ(T ← 0) in the energy basis of the initial field is the identity, and that one record in the energy basis erases the fringe

1. Which Phases Are Gauge?¶

A particle hops around a ring of $N = 6$ sites; the hop from site $k$ to $k+1$ carries a phase $e^{i\varphi_k}$. In electromagnetism these phases come from the vector potential, and their sum $\Phi = \sum_k \varphi_k$ is the magnetic flux through the ring.

Three ways to change phases:

  • Barandes's Schur–Hadamard gauge transformation (Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes, §3.2): multiply each entry of the evolution matrix $\Theta(t \leftarrow 0)$ by its own phase. $\Gamma = |\Theta|^2$ cannot change, by construction.
  • An electromagnetic gauge transformation: rephase each site, $H \to DHD^\dagger$. Then $\Theta \to D\Theta D^\dagger$: a Schur–Hadamard transformation with $\theta_{ij} = g_i - g_j$. Moving the flux from one bond to another is one of these. This is immediate from his eq. 30: a site rephasing is a diagonal $V$ in Barandes's dynamical-symmetry condition (Paper 3 §4.1, eq. 87), which every diagonal $V$ satisfies, and the same map is his Foldy–Wouthuysen gauge transformation (§3.6, eq. 74, with $V = D$). Barandes introduces his gauge by analogy with classical electromagnetism (§3.2) and does not discuss the lattice case.
  • Changing the flux itself.

3. Can Γ Tell the Sign of the Flux?¶

Reversing the flux is time reversal, so $\Gamma(-\Phi) = \Gamma(\Phi)^{\mathsf T}$ exactly. If $\Gamma$ were symmetric it could not tell $\Phi$ from $-\Phi$. Measure the asymmetry $\max|\Gamma - \Gamma^{\mathsf T}|$ on rings of different sizes.

On an even ring, flipping the sign of every other site is a gauge transformation taking $H$ to $-H$, and $\Gamma(-H) = \Gamma(H)^{\mathsf T}$, so Γ is symmetric for every flux (the printout checks four fluxes; the annotated copy gives the argument in full).

4. Records Erase the Flux¶

First dense records (notebook 11's division events every $\tau$, over a stretch of $1/\tau^2$ steps). Then weak records: after each step every coherence between configurations is multiplied by $1 - \varepsilon$, from $\varepsilon = 0$ (no record) to $\varepsilon = 1$ (a full division event).

5. Berry's Phase¶

A spin-1/2 sits in a magnetic field of fixed strength $B_0$ that tilts at angle $\theta$ from the $z$ axis and goes once around $z$ in time $T$. The configurations are spin up and spin down along $z$. Griffiths (§10.2.3) shows that each energy state picks up a geometric phase of minus half the solid angle swept by the field, $\Omega = 2\pi(1 - \cos\theta)$, the two states with opposite signs.

Three questions for Γ:

  • In the energy basis of the initial (= final) field, what does Γ(T ← 0) look like after a slow loop?
  • In the configuration basis, can Γ recover $\Omega$? A single Γ entry mixes Berry's phase with the much larger dynamical phase, so compare against a reference run with a static field of the same strength and duration, scanning $B_0T$: the loop shifts the fringe.
  • What does one record in the energy basis, halfway round, do?

The test angles are chosen so that $\Omega$ mod $2\pi$ is neither 0 nor π. At $\theta = \pi/2$ the solid angle is $2\pi$, which shifts nothing; at $\pi/3$ and $2\pi/3$ it is π, where a shift of $+\Omega$ and $-\Omega$ look the same. All four test angles, $\pi/6$, $\pi/4$, $3\pi/8$ and $3\pi/4$, test both the size and the sign of the shift (for each, $\Omega$ and $-\Omega$ differ mod $2\pi$); $3\pi/4$ also tests a cone below the equator, where $\Omega > 2\pi$.

6. A Non-Abelian Flux: SU(2) Links (added 2026-11-11)¶

Give the particle a two-valued internal label, "colour", and let each hop multiply it by a 2×2 SU(2) matrix instead of a phase. The configurations are (site, colour). Three checks:

  • Is an SU(2) gauge transformation (rotating colour at each site) a Schur–Hadamard gauge? In Barandes's framework this is the dynamical-symmetry test of Paper 3 §4.1 (eqs. 85–87): with the configuration projectors held fixed, $V\Theta V^\dagger$ describes the same process if and only if it differs from Θ by a Schur–Hadamard transformation. (If the projectors are rotated too, it is his Foldy–Wouthuysen gauge transformation, §3.6 eq. 74, which leaves Γ unchanged by construction.) Holding the colour basis fixed as the configurations, compare Γ before and after.
  • One ring. The loop product is now a matrix $W$, with eigenphases $\pm\varphi$. Does Γ on the sites (colour averaged) see the orientation of the loop, as an abelian flux does on odd rings?
  • Two loops. With each loop's trace held fixed, does Γ see the trace of the product $W_1W_2$, which has no abelian counterpart?

def link_H(n_sites, edges, links):
    d = links[0].shape[0]
    H = np.zeros((n_sites * d, n_sites * d), complex)
    for (i, j), U in zip(edges, links):

Summary¶

  • Γ sees phases only around loops. Arbitrary phases on the entries of the evolution matrix are gauge (Barandes's Schur–Hadamard gauge); lattice electromagnetic gauge transformations are the special case $\theta_{ij} = g_i - g_j$ (immediate from his eq. 30: a diagonal $V$ in his §4.1 condition, eq. 87, and his Foldy–Wouthuysen gauge, §3.6, with $V = D$; Barandes introduces his gauge by analogy with classical electromagnetism, §3.2, and does not discuss the lattice case). What is left is the flux, the loop product of the hopping phases, and it changes Γ. This is the lattice version of Peskin's statement that gauge-invariant functions of the potential are built from Wilson loops.
  • The Aharonov–Bohm effect is one entry of Γ. At the opposite site of a 6-site ring, $\Gamma_{30}(\Phi)/\Gamma_{30}(0) = \cos^2(\Phi/2)$ at short times (to 4 decimals at both printed times); at $\Phi = \pi$, $\Gamma_{30}$ vanishes at all times (the $\Phi = \pi$ curve in the plot stays at zero). The particle's configurations never include the flux.
  • The sign of the flux is visible only on odd rings. $\Gamma(-\Phi) = \Gamma(\Phi)^{\mathsf T}$ always; on even rings Γ is symmetric for every flux (the sublattice sign flip, section 3), the Γ(H) = Γ(−H) blindness of notebook 12.
  • Records erase the flux. Dense records give a flux-blind random walk; weak records reduce the Aharonov–Bohm visibility smoothly from 1 to 0.
  • Berry's phase. In the energy basis of the initial (= final) field, Γ(T ← 0) is the identity after a slow loop, blind to Berry's phase. (Only in the instantaneous energy basis, which turns with the field, is Γ(t ← 0) ≈ 1 at every t; in the fixed basis Γ(T/2 ← 0) has off-diagonal ≈ sin²θ, so the process is not divisible at T/2.) In the configuration basis the loop shifts the fringe by the solid angle Ω, approaching it as $1/T$ (at $T = 2000$: 0.836, 1.828, 3.858, 4.431 against Ω mod 2π = 0.842, 1.840, 3.879, 4.443). One record in the energy basis halfway round erases the fringe (amplitude 0.225 → 0.013; the remainder is non-adiabatic), not only Berry's shift: the same record on a static field erases its fringe too. That is a property of records, not of Berry's phase.
  • Non-abelian links (section 6). Rotating colour site by site fails Barandes's dynamical-symmetry condition (Paper 3 §4.1, eq. 86) when colour is a configuration: it changes the colour-resolved Γ (by up to 0.27 for this random draw; 0.54 in the spike's), while the colour-averaged site Γ is unchanged. So if local SU(2) rotations are to be a redundancy, as in lattice gauge theory, colour cannot serve as a configuration; only colour-blind quantities can. An SU(2) loop cannot show its orientation even on an odd ring (every SU(2) matrix is equivalent to its inverse), and with two loops Γ sees the trace of their product. All of this is standard non-abelian gauge physics restated in Γ language.
  • So the phases Γ can see are loop phases, and it sees them only through interference within indivisible stretches, the same pattern as the exchange interference of notebook 14.

Caveats: one particle, small rings, one spin; the fringe comparison needs a reference run, as Griffiths' beam-splitter argument does.

Prior work: that loop phases are the only gauge invariants of the hopping phases, and that Γ(−Φ) = Γ(Φ)ᵀ with no sign information on even (bipartite) rings, are known from the chiral-quantum-walk literature (Zimborás et al. (2013); Lu et al., arXiv:1405.6209). The cos²(Φ/2) visibility is the textbook Aharonov–Bohm result; records erasing it is Stern, Aharonov and Imry (1990); the Berry fringe is Berry (1984). Antonakos and Terzis (2023) treat Aharonov–Bohm in Nelson's stochastic mechanics.
