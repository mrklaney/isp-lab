Notebook 06: Division Events — How Their Spacing Shapes the Dynamics¶

Goal: Derive division events from an environment that records the system, then vary the time between records and watch the same qubit move from indivisible, to divisible (a Markov chain at the record times), to nearly frozen.

By the end of this notebook you will be able to:

  • Derive a division event from a full system-plus-environment unitary, with no extra postulate
  • Predict the dynamics for any record spacing δt from one number, ωδt
  • Locate the spacing below which the process is divisible over every interval
  • Show that imperfect records no longer guarantee division events, and measure when they fail

Sources. "Paper 1, 2, 3" are Jacob A. Barandes's papers:

  • Paper 1: "The Stochastic-Quantum Correspondence", arXiv:2302.10778 (Philosophy of Physics 3(1), 8, 2025). §3.7, "Division events and the Markov approximation", is the section this notebook simulates.
  • Paper 2: "The Stochastic-Quantum Theorem", arXiv:2309.03085.
  • Paper 3: "Quantum Systems as Indivisible Stochastic Processes", arXiv:2507.21192 (Stud. Hist. Phil. Sci. 119, 102213, 2026).

Other works are cited in the text by author and arXiv number or journal reference.

1. Division Events From an Eavesdropping Environment¶

System: a qubit with configurations 0 and 1 and $H = \frac{\omega}{2}\sigma_x$. Time is in units of $1/\omega$.

Environment: a supply of fresh qubits, all starting in 0. At each time $t = k\,\delta t$, environment qubit $k$ records the system's configuration: it is left in $|e_0\rangle$ if the system is in 0 and $|e_1\rangle$ if it is in 1. The record quality is the overlap $c = \langle e_0 | e_1 \rangle$: $c = 0$ is a perfect record, $c = 1$ is no record at all.

Paper 1, §3.7, eqs. 57–58: with perfect records at spacing $\delta t$,

Gamma(n dt <- 0) = Gamma_dt ^ n,     Gamma_dt = |U(dt)|^2

which is a discrete-time Markov chain. Between records the qubit's own dynamics takes over:

Gamma(t <- 0) = |U(t - k dt)|^2  Gamma_dt ^ k,     k = floor(t / dt)

Every matrix here has the form $\Gamma = \frac12\begin{pmatrix} 1+\lambda & 1-\lambda \\ 1-\lambda & 1+\lambda \end{pmatrix}$, so one number $\lambda(t)$ (the gap between the "started in 0" and "started in 1" columns) describes the whole process. The closed form below is checked against a full unitary simulation of system plus environment.

The construction is Paper 1's own (§3.7). This section reproduces it; what it adds is the explicit simulation.

OMEGA = 1.0


def U(tau):
    # Qubit unitary for H = (OMEGA/2) sigma_x
    c, s = np.cos(OMEGA * tau / 2), np.sin(OMEGA * tau / 2)
    return np.array([[c, -1j * s], [-1j * s, c]])


def bloch(t, dt, c=0.0):
    # Bloch components (y, z) of the system started in 0, records every dt with overlap c.
    # Between records: rotation about x. At a record: coherence (y) multiplied by c.
    t = np.atleast_1d(np.asarray(t, float))
    if np.isinf(dt):
        return -np.sin(OMEGA * t), np.cos(OMEGA * t)
    k = np.floor(t / dt + 1e-9).astype(int)
    y_rec, z_rec = [0.0], [1.0]
    phi = OMEGA * dt
    for _ in range(k.max()):
        y, z = y_rec[-1], z_rec[-1]
        y_rec.append(c * (y * np.cos(phi) - z * np.sin(phi)))
        z_rec.append(y * np.sin(phi) + z * np.cos(phi))
    y0, z0, phi_t = np.array(y_rec)[k], np.array(z_rec)[k], OMEGA * (t - k * dt)
    return y0 * np.cos(phi_t) - z0 * np.sin(phi_t), y0 * np.sin(phi_t) + z0 * np.cos(phi_t)


def stretch(t, dt, c=0.0):
    # lambda(t) = Gamma_00(t <- 0) - Gamma_01(t <- 0)
    return bloch(t, dt, c)[1]


def gamma(t, dt, c=0.0):
    lam = stretch(t, dt, c)[0]
    return np.array([[1 + lam, 1 - lam], [1 - lam, 1 + lam]]) / 2


def gamma_environment(t, dt, n_env, c=0.0):
    # Gamma_S(t <- 0) from the full unitary on system + n_env environment qubits, then
    # marginalizing over environment configurations. Record k rotates environment qubit k
    # by Ry(theta) when the system is in 1, with cos(theta/2) = c.
    cr, sr = c, np.sqrt(1 - c ** 2)
    k = min(int(np.floor(t / dt + 1e-9)), n_env)
    G = np.zeros((2, 2))
    for j in (0, 1):
        psi = np.zeros((2,) * (1 + n_env), complex)
        psi[(j,) + (0,) * n_env] = 1
        for m in range(k):
            psi = np.tensordot(U(dt), psi, axes=([1], [0]))
            a0, a1 = psi[1].take(0, axis=m), psi[1].take(1, axis=m)
            psi[1] = np.stack([cr * a0 - sr * a1, sr * a0 + cr * a1], axis=m)
        psi = np.tensordot(U(t - k * dt), psi, axes=([1], [0]))
        G[:, j] = (np.abs(psi) ** 2).reshape(2, -1).sum(axis=1)
    return G


def divides(t_prime, t, dt, c=0.0):
    # Inverse-free test: does ANY valid stochastic Gamma(t <- t') exist?
    proc = PairwiseProcess(states=['0', '1'], times=[0.0, t_prime, t],
                           matrices={(0, 1): gamma(t_prime, dt, c), (0, 2): gamma(t, dt, c)})
    return check_divisibility_lp(proc)['divisible']

3. Forgetting Rate Versus Spacing¶

Each record multiplies the envelope by $|\cos\omega\delta t|$, so the relaxation rate is

$$\gamma(\delta t) = -\frac{\ln|\cos\omega\delta t|}{\delta t} \;\approx\; \frac{\omega^2\,\delta t}{2} \quad (\omega\delta t \ll 1).$$

Three landmarks:

  • $\omega\delta t \to 0$: $\gamma \to 0$. Frequent perfect records freeze the configuration: the quantum Zeno effect (Misra and Sudarshan, 1977). $\gamma$ is the population-relaxation form of the repeated-measurement Zeno rate: twice the survival-probability rate $\omega^2\delta t/4$, because $\lambda$ is a population difference. Itano et al. (1990) measured the same $\cos^n$ product in a driven two-level ion.
  • $\omega\delta t = \pi/2$: $\gamma = \infty$. Each record lands on a coin flip; one record erases the start.
  • $\omega\delta t = \pi$: $\gamma = 0$. Each record lands when $|U(\delta t)|^2$ is a permutation. The records are transparent and the process is identical to the closed one. (Pimenta, arXiv:2505.08785, §V.3, notes that every passage of $\Gamma$ through a permutation matrix is a "proper" division event.)

4. Where Divisibility Holds¶

For this family, a valid $\Gamma(t \leftarrow t')$ exists exactly when $|\lambda(t)| \le |\lambda(t')|$: a stochastic step cannot increase the distance between two distributions. (This rule needs no inverse, so it also covers singular $\Gamma(t' \leftarrow 0)$.)

Records make every $k\,\delta t$ a conditioning time. Between records $|\lambda|$ follows $|\cos\omega\tau|$, which is non-increasing for $\omega\tau \le \pi/2$. Hence:

  • $\omega\delta t \le \pi/2$: $|\lambda|$ never increases, and the process is divisible over every pair of times.
  • $\omega\delta t > \pi/2$: $|\lambda|$ revives inside each interval, and indivisible pairs return.

Prior work. The threshold is known. The gap rule is the two-state case of Buscemi and Datta's theorem that a process is divisible exactly when distinguishability never increases (Buscemi–Datta 2016, arXiv:1408.7062), and the $\pi/2$ threshold follows from it and from Pimenta's two-configuration analysis (arXiv:2505.08785).

5. Imperfect Records¶

With overlap $c > 0$ a record only partly separates the environment's branches. The system's coherence is multiplied by $c$ at each record instead of being erased, and the record times are no longer guaranteed to be division events.

Measure the failure directly: after each record time $k\,\delta t$, how much distinguishability comes back,

$$R = \max_{k}\;\max_{t > k\delta t}\Big(|\lambda(t)| - |\lambda(k\,\delta t)|\Big)_{+}.$$

$R = 0$ means a valid $\Gamma(t \leftarrow k\,\delta t)$ exists from every record time checked; with perfect records ($c = 0$) the record times are exact division events. $R > 0$ means some record time is not an exact division event. Since $|\lambda(t)|$ is the Kolmogorov distance between the runs started in 0 and in 1, $R$ is the maximal revival of that distance after a record time: the classical memory witness of Smirne, Stabile and Vacchini (arXiv:1211.5913), evaluated after record times.

Prior work. Barandes notes that division events are "generically always approximate" and become nearly exact when the environment is sufficiently macroscopic (Paper 1, §3.7 and fn. 18, p. 19). Paper 3 (§2.2, p. 10) adds that they may be "generated to an extremely good approximation through interactions with other systems." Whether a time is a division event is a property of $\Gamma$ alone (Paper 2, eq. 35). Approximate division events, valid up to a stated level of precision, are treated by Barandes, Hasan and Kagan (arXiv:2512.18105, §3.1). Measures of divisibility stated in $\Gamma$ alone exist (Buscemi–Datta 2016, arXiv:1408.7062; Smirne, Stabile and Vacchini 2013, arXiv:1211.5913). Calvo (arXiv:2601.18720, §5.1) ties exactness to a one-to-one match between environment and system configurations, and Smirne et al. (2019, arXiv:1709.05267) give a related criterion for when repeated-measurement statistics are classical. Not found in our searches: a physical sufficient condition on system plus environment, stated in transition probabilities, that guarantees approximate division events at a given precision.

def revival(dt, c, n_records=8, t_max=30.0):
    worst = 0.0
    for k in range(1, n_records + 1):

Summary¶

  • A recording environment produces division events with no extra postulate: the full unitary on system plus environment, marginalized, gives a transition matrix that divides at every record time. This reproduces Paper 1, §3.7.
  • With perfect records every $\delta t$ the process is a Markov chain at the record times (Paper 1, eq. 57), and one number, $\omega\delta t$, sets its behaviour.
  • $\omega\delta t \le \pi/2$: divisible over every pair of times. $\omega\delta t > \pi/2$: indivisible between records.
  • The relaxation rate $-\ln|\cos\omega\delta t|/\delta t$ is not monotone in the record rate. It vanishes as $\delta t \to 0$ (Zeno), diverges at $\omega\delta t = \pi/2$, and vanishes again at $\omega\delta t = \pi$, where the records are transparent.
  • Imperfect records ($c > 0$) no longer guarantee division events. Record times fail to divide ($R > 0$) once $c$ is large enough for the spacing, though short spacings tolerate fairly blurry records. Paper 1 (fn. 18) anticipates that division events are generically approximate.

Caveat: the Zeno freezing follows from ideal, instantaneous, configuration-basis records in this two-state model. How smooth classical motion survives very frequent, realistic (coarse-grained, imperfect) records is not addressed here.

Prior work. None of the results here is new. Records making division events is Paper 1's own construction (§3.7). The forgetting rate is the population-relaxation form of the repeated-measurement Zeno rate (Misra and Sudarshan, 1977; Itano et al., 1990). The $\omega\delta t \le \pi/2$ threshold follows from Buscemi and Datta's divisibility criterion (Buscemi–Datta 2016) and from Pimenta (arXiv:2505.08785). The revival $R$ is Smirne, Stabile and Vacchini's Kolmogorov-distance witness (arXiv:1211.5913). That division events are approximate is Barandes's own point (Paper 1, fn. 18), and approximate division events at a stated precision appear in Barandes, Hasan and Kagan (arXiv:2512.18105, §3.1). What the notebook adds is a worked, fully simulated example.
