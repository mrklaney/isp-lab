Notebook 07: An Energy–Time Uncertainty Relation Written Only in Γ¶

Goal: State an energy–time uncertainty relation using nothing but the transition matrices Γ(t←0), and turn it into a speed limit on how fast an indivisible process can recover distinguishability that a divisible process could only lose.

By the end of this notebook you will be able to:

  • Read a configuration's "agility" ν directly off the curvature of Γ at t = 0
  • State three inequalities in which only Γ appears, and check them numerically
  • Show that two of the three follow from the first by geometry on Γ alone
  • Identify where the bounds need a time-homogeneous (fixed-$H$) law, and how they can fail without one

No ħ anywhere¶

Every quantity in this notebook is built from Γ. The "energy" that appears is the agility $\nu_j$, read off Γ, with units of 1/time. Every inequality compares $\nu \cdot t$, a pure number, with another pure number. Planck's constant never enters, in the statements or in the code.

ħ would appear only if you chose to convert ν into joules, $\Delta E_j = \hbar\,\nu_j$. Then the single-run ($n = 1$) form of inequality 1 below becomes $\delta t\,\Delta E \ge \hbar/2$, the parameter-estimation form of the energy–time relation. That conversion is a choice of units, not physics.

Speed limits stated without ħ are not new in themselves: they are known for classical dynamics (Okuyama & Ohzeki, PRL 120, 070402, 2018; Shanahan, Chenu, Margolus & del Campo, PRL 120, 070401, 2018).

1. Reading the Agility ν Off Γ¶

Throughout, a time-homogeneous unistochastic process means one generated by a single fixed Hermitian matrix $H$ (units 1/time): $\Gamma(t \leftarrow 0) = |e^{-iHt}|^2$, entry by entry. The law does not change with time, but the process is in general indivisible: $\Gamma(t \leftarrow 0)$ is not a Markov semigroup. Expanding,

$$\Gamma_{jj}(t \leftarrow 0) = 1 - \nu_j^2\, t^2 + O(t^4), \qquad\text{so}\qquad \nu_j \equiv \sqrt{-\tfrac12\,\ddot\Gamma_{jj}(0)}.$$

$\nu_j$ is defined by Γ alone. It equals the standard deviation of $H$ in configuration $j$, which is checked below. The code never uses it; the proof of inequality 1 does. Reading ν off the $t^2$ start of the survival probability $\Gamma_{jj}$ is standard: $1/\nu_j$ is the Zeno time $\tau_Z$ of Facchi & Pascazio (quant-ph/0101044). A Markov semigroup (constant rates) instead leaves linearly, $\Gamma_{jj} = 1 - r_j t + \dots$, so $\dot\Gamma_{jj}(0) \neq 0$ and no finite ν exists for configurations with a nonzero exit rate. A divisible process has a finite ν only if its rates vanish at $t = 0$, like notebook 03's Gaussian before $t^*$.

rng = np.random.default_rng(7)


def random_H(N):
    A = rng.normal(size=(N, N)) + 1j * rng.normal(size=(N, N))
    return (A + A.conj().T) / 2                       # Hermitian, units of 1/time


def unitary_process(H):
    return lambda t: unitary_to_transition_matrix(expm(-1j * H * t))


def markov_process(L):
    return lambda t: expm(L * t)


def agility(G, h=1e-4):
    # nu_j = sqrt(-Gamma_jj''(0)/2); Gamma_jj is even in t, so (Gamma_jj(h) - 1)/h^2 = -nu_j^2
    return np.sqrt(np.clip(-(np.diag(G(h)) - 1) / h ** 2, 0, None))


def fisher(G, t, h=1e-5):
    # Fisher information about t carried by each column of Gamma(t <- 0)
    P, dP = G(t), (G(t + h) - G(t - h)) / (2 * h)
    with np.errstate(divide='ignore', invalid='ignore'):
        return np.where(P > 1e-10, dP ** 2 / P, 0.0).sum(axis=0)


def angle(P):
    # Bhattacharyya angle between every pair of columns: arccos sum_i sqrt(P_ij P_ik)
    return np.arccos(np.clip(np.sqrt(P).T @ np.sqrt(P), -1, 1))


def tv(P):
    # total-variation distance between every pair of columns
    return 0.5 * np.abs(P[:, :, None] - P[:, None, :]).sum(axis=0)

2. Inequality 1: Clock Precision¶

Started in configuration $j$, the configuration found at time $t$ is a clock reading. The information it carries about $t$ is the Fisher information of column $j$:

$$I_j(t) = \sum_i \frac{\dot\Gamma_{ij}(t \leftarrow 0)^2}{\Gamma_{ij}(t \leftarrow 0)} \;\le\; 4\,\nu_j^2 .$$

By the Cramér–Rao bound, any unbiased estimate of $t$ from $n$ observed runs has spread $\delta t \ge 1/\sqrt{n\,I_j}$, so

$$\delta t \cdot \nu_j \;\ge\; \frac{1}{2\sqrt n}.$$

This is an energy–time uncertainty relation, in its parameter-estimation form (Braunstein–Caves–Milburn), with Γ on both sides and no ħ. Equality holds as $t \to 0$. The qubit saturates it at every $t$. A Markov process has $I_j(t) \to \infty$ as $t \to 0$, so it obeys no such bound.

This is the one step whose proof is borrowed (classical Fisher information ≤ quantum Fisher information, Braunstein–Caves 1994). Everything after it uses Γ alone.

Prior work. The bound is known. Braunstein, Caves and Milburn (1996, quant-ph/9507004) state this clock bound for the statistics of a fixed measurement, with the energy spread as the agility. Here it is only rewritten with ν read off Γ.

3. Inequality 2: A Speed Limit on Recovering Distinguishability¶

Measure how distinguishable starting configurations $j$ and $k$ are at time $t$ by the angle between columns,

$$\theta_{jk}(t) = \arccos \sum_i \sqrt{\Gamma_{ij}(t \leftarrow 0)\,\Gamma_{ik}(t \leftarrow 0)} \;\in [0, \pi/2].$$

  • Divisible at $t'$ ($\Gamma(t \leftarrow t')$ stochastic for every $t > t'$): $\theta_{jk}$ cannot increase after $t'$ (a stochastic map cannot decrease the Bhattacharyya coefficient).
  • Indivisible, with a fixed $H$: $\theta_{jk}$ can increase, which is recovery, but never faster than

$$\big|\dot\theta_{jk}(t)\big| \;\le\; \nu_j + \nu_k .$$

Derivation from inequality 1, using only Γ. The map $p \mapsto \sqrt p$ puts every column on the unit sphere, where $\theta_{jk}$ is the great-circle distance. Column $j$ moves on that sphere at speed $\tfrac12\sqrt{I_j} \le \nu_j$. Two points whose speeds are at most $\nu_j$ and $\nu_k$ cannot separate faster than $\nu_j + \nu_k$.

Prior work. This is a one-line corollary of the Fisher-information speed limits of Nishiyama and Hasegawa (Phys. Rev. E 114, 014120, 2026; arXiv:2504.04790), applied to two columns at once.

Corollary for the distance used in notebooks 03 and 06. Total variation obeys $D_{jk} \le \sin\theta_{jk}$, so

$$D_{jk}(t) \;\le\; \sin\!\Big(\min\big\{\tfrac{\pi}{2},\; \theta_{jk}(t') + (\nu_j + \nu_k)(t - t')\big\}\Big) \qquad \text{for } t \ge t'.$$

4. Inequality 3: Leaving a Configuration Takes Time¶

Apply the same sphere argument to column $j$ and its own starting point $e_j$. The angle between them is $\arccos\sqrt{\Gamma_{jj}}$, which grows no faster than $\nu_j$:

$$\Gamma_{jj}(t \leftarrow 0) \;\ge\; \cos^2(\nu_j t) \qquad \text{for } \nu_j t \le \pi/2 .$$

This is the Mandelstam–Tamm bound, written in Γ and derived here from inequality 1.

Corollary. Call an intrinsic division event (this lab's term) a time at which Γ becomes a permutation, so that every configuration lands on a definite configuration and the process divides there with no environment. Barandes (Paper 1, §3.7) introduces division events through interaction with an environment instead. A permutation needs $\Gamma_{jj} = 0$ for every configuration that moves, so it cannot occur before $\max_j \pi/(2\nu_j)$, the maximum over the configurations that move. For the qubit that is $\omega t = \pi$, the "transparent records" spacing of notebook 06.

5. Where the Bounds Need a Fixed Law¶

ν is read at $t = 0$, so the bounds need $I_j(t) \le 4\nu_j^2$ at all $t$, which a fixed $H$ guarantees. Two tests:

  • Gaussian (notebook 03). Not of the form $|e^{-iHt}|^2$. It is a 2×2 rotation with $\cos^2\phi = e^{-t^2}$, and $\phi$ never moves faster than at $t=0$, so it obeys the bound.
  • An accelerating rotation, $\Gamma = \begin{pmatrix}\cos^2\phi & \sin^2\phi\\ \sin^2\phi & \cos^2\phi\end{pmatrix}$ with $\phi = t + t^3$. Valid and indivisible, with ν = 1 at $t = 0$, but it later moves much faster.

Summary¶

For a time-homogeneous unistochastic process, with $\nu_j = \sqrt{-\tfrac12\ddot\Gamma_{jj}(0)}$ read off Γ:

  1. Clock precision: $\sum_i \dot\Gamma_{ij}^2/\Gamma_{ij} \le 4\nu_j^2$, hence $\delta t \cdot \nu_j \ge 1/(2\sqrt n)$. An energy–time uncertainty relation, in its parameter-estimation form.
  2. Recovery speed limit: $|\dot\theta_{jk}| \le \nu_j + \nu_k$. Divisible processes cannot recover distinguishability at all; indivisible ones with a fixed $H$ can, at a bounded rate. For total variation, $D_{jk}(t) \le \sin\!\big(\min\{\tfrac\pi2,\ \theta_{jk}(t') + (\nu_j+\nu_k)(t-t')\}\big)$ for $t \ge t'$.
  3. Leaving takes time: $\Gamma_{jj}(t) \ge \cos^2(\nu_j t)$. An intrinsic division event (Γ a permutation) cannot occur before $\max_j \pi/(2\nu_j)$ over the configurations that move.

Inequalities 2 and 3 follow from 1 by geometry on Γ alone. Inequality 1 is the single borrowed fact; its proof currently goes through Hilbert space (Braunstein–Caves). The qubit saturates all three. Markov semigroups (constant rates) have no finite ν; a divisible process has one only if its rates vanish at $t = 0$. The bounds require a law that does not speed up after $t = 0$; an accelerating (time-dependent) law breaks them.

Prior work. None of the three bounds is new. Inequality 1, with ν as the energy spread, is the clock bound of Braunstein, Caves and Milburn (1996), built on Braunstein–Caves (1994). Inequality 2 is a one-line corollary of Nishiyama and Hasegawa's Fisher-information speed limits (2026). Inequality 3 is the Mandelstam–Tamm bound. Reading ν off the $t^2$ start of $\Gamma_{jj}$ is the standard Zeno time, $\tau_Z = 1/\nu$ (Facchi & Pascazio, quant-ph/0101044), and speed limits without ħ are known for classical dynamics (Okuyama & Ohzeki 2018; Shanahan et al. 2018). What is specific here is only the recovery bound read as a limit on indivisibility.

No ħ appears anywhere. ν has units of 1/time and comes from Γ. Calling $\hbar\nu$ an energy is a units convention.
