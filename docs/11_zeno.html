Notebook 11: Zeno and Anti-Zeno¶

Goal: Watch a decaying system with repeated division events, and find in Γ when watching slows the decay (Zeno) and when it speeds it up (anti-Zeno).

By the end of this notebook you will be able to:

  • Run a decay with a division event every τ, as Barandes's chain $\Gamma(\tau)^n$, and read off its effective rate
  • Show that frequent events freeze the decay at the rate $\nu^2\tau$, set by notebook 07's agility alone
  • Show that when the level sits away from where the band couples most strongly, watching can nearly triple the decay rate
  • State the criterion in Γ: Zeno if survival at τ lies above the exponential it eventually follows, anti-Zeno if below, and see why notebook 10's single-event test is not the same thing
  • Measure how strong the records must be: weak, frequent records act as dephasing at a rate $\gamma_\varphi$, and freeze the decay only once $\gamma_\varphi$ is comparable to the bandwidth

1. Watching a Decay¶

Notebook 10's flat band: one level $b$ coupled to $M$ band levels, golden-rule rate κ, bandwidth $W$. Now an environment records the configuration every τ. Each record is a division event, so the process restarts from the recorded configuration, and (Barandes, Correspondence §3.7, eqs. 57–58)

$$\Gamma(n\tau\leftarrow 0) = \Gamma(\tau)^n, \qquad \Gamma(\tau) = |U(\tau)|^2 .$$

A Markov chain, built from one indivisible step. The κ used throughout is read off Γ: the slope of $\ln P(t)$ at late times.

2. Zeno: Frequent Events Freeze the Decay¶

With an event every τ, and neglecting re-excitation from the band (under 1% here, section 1), survival after $n$ events is $P(\tau)^n = e^{-\gamma_{\rm eff}\, n\tau}$, so the effective rate is

$$\gamma_{\rm eff}(\tau) = -\frac{\ln P(\tau)}{\tau}.$$

At short times $P(\tau) \approx 1 - \nu^2\tau^2$ (notebook 10), so $\gamma_{\rm eff} \approx \nu^2\tau \to 0$. At long τ, $P(\tau) \approx Z e^{-\kappa\tau}$, so $\gamma_{\rm eff} \approx \kappa - \ln Z/\tau$.

3. Anti-Zeno: Watching Can Speed It Up¶

Now make the band structured: the coupling $g_k^2$ is a Lorentzian peaked at energy 2 (half-width 0.5), while the level $b$ sits at energy 0, out in the tail. The golden rule only sees the weak coupling at $b$'s own energy. But over an interval τ between records, transitions need conserve energy only to within about $2\pi/\tau$, and once that window reaches the peak, decay speeds up.

4. The Criterion, in Γ¶

Neglecting re-excitation from the band, survival after $n$ events is $P(\tau)^n$; without events it eventually follows $Z e^{-\kappa n\tau}$. So the long-run verdict depends on one comparison:

$$\textbf{Zeno at interval }\tau \iff P(\tau) > e^{-\kappa\tau}, \qquad \textbf{anti-Zeno} \iff P(\tau) < e^{-\kappa\tau}.$$

Watching helps exactly when survival at τ is ahead of the exponential it eventually follows. Both sides are read off Γ. At long τ the comparison reduces to the intercept: $\gamma_{\rm eff} \approx \kappa - \ln Z/\tau$, so $Z > 1$ means Zeno and $Z < 1$ means anti-Zeno. This is the criterion of Facchi, Nakazato and Pascazio, Phys. Rev. Lett. 86, 2699 (2001), eqs. 2–4, restated in Facchi and Pascazio (2001), §3, together with the role of $Z$ (their Figs. 3–4): the crossover interval is where $P(t)$ meets $e^{-\kappa t}$. What Γ adds is that both sides are entries of one transition matrix.

Notebook 10 asked a different question: does one event at τ raise survival at $2\tau$, i.e. is $P(\tau)^2 > P(2\tau)$? The table compares the two. The last lines check two things: which κ to use, and whether the flat band's $P(2\tau)$ at τ = 100 is still on its exponential.

5. How Strong Must the Records Be?¶

So far every record was perfect. A weaker record only partly divides the process: each one multiplies the off-diagonal elements of the density matrix (in the configuration basis) by $1 - \varepsilon$. That is notebook 06's imperfect record and notebook 08's phase damping. $\varepsilon = 1$ is a full division event; $\varepsilon = 0$ is no record. Frequent weak records act like steady dephasing at the rate

$$\gamma_\varphi = -\frac{\ln(1-\varepsilon)}{\tau}.$$

The prediction to test comes from Kofman and Kurizki's (2001) dephasing result. Each band level is fed at the rate $2g^2\gamma_\varphi/(\varepsilon_k^2+\gamma_\varphi^2)$, and summing over the flat band gives $\gamma_{\rm eff} = \kappa\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan\!\big(W/2\gamma_\varphi\big)$: no Zeno while $\gamma_\varphi \ll W$, and freezing as $2\nu^2/\gamma_\varphi$ once $\gamma_\varphi \gg W$.

def rate_with_records(tau, eps, t1=5.0, t2=20.0):
    s = survival_with_records(small, tau, eps, int(round(t2 / tau)))
    return -np.log(s[-1] / s[int(round(t1 / tau))]) / (t2 - t1)

k_small = rate_with_records(0.5, 0.0)
print(f"no records: rate {k_small:.4f}  (M = 250 band, golden rule {small.rate:.4f})\n")

Summary¶

  • Watching is a chain of division events: $\Gamma(n\tau) = \Gamma(\tau)^n$ (Barandes eq. 57). For decay it acts on survival alone, $P(\tau)^n$, to better than 1% here.
  • Zeno. In a flat band, $\gamma_{\rm eff} = \nu^2\tau$ for $\tau \lesssim 1/W$: frequent events freeze the decay at a rate set by notebook 07's agility (a frequency, in this lab's units). For longer τ the rate approaches κ from below, as $\kappa - \ln Z/\tau$ with $Z > 1$.
  • Anti-Zeno. When the level sits in the tail of a structured band, watching at τ ≈ 1 makes it decay nearly three times faster. Its intercept is $Z < 1$, so watching speeds it up at long τ. Numerically it does so for every τ above about 0.23 and slows it only below that.
  • The criterion is stated in Γ: Zeno iff $P(\tau) > e^{-\kappa\tau}$, with κ read off Γ, i.e. iff survival at τ is ahead of its eventual exponential. Notebook 10's single-event test is a different question; the two genuinely disagree for the structured band at τ = 0.3. The flat band's mismatch at τ = 100 is not physics: $P(2\tau)$ is then $2.45\times10^{-9}$, in the late-time tail of this finite band (33% above $Ze^{-\kappa t}$), and the one-event sign is set by a difference of $6\times10^{-10}$. κ must be read off Γ: the golden-rule κ = 0.1 would label the flat band anti-Zeno at τ = 30 and 100. (Both the 0.6% and $Z>1$ are the band-edge self-energy, $Z\approx(1-4g^2/\delta W)^{-2}$.)
  • Imperfect records. Weak, frequent records act as dephasing at $\gamma_\varphi = -\ln(1-\varepsilon)/\tau$ and give $\gamma_{\rm eff} \approx \kappa\cdot\frac{2}{\pi}\arctan(W/2\gamma_\varphi)$ when $\gamma_\varphi \gg \kappa$. Freezing needs $\gamma_\varphi$ comparable to $W$.

Caveats: one flat and one Lorentzian band; the arctan formula borrows a rate-equation step and fails when $\gamma_\varphi$ is comparable to κ; κ for the structured band is 6% below its golden-rule estimate (about two-thirds of it from the level shift), which is why κ is read off Γ.

Prior work: the criterion is Facchi, Nakazato and Pascazio, Phys. Rev. Lett. 86, 2699 (2001), eqs. 2–4, restated almost verbatim in Facchi and Pascazio (2001), §3, including the long-τ role of $Z$; the freezing rate $\nu^2\tau$ is the textbook Zeno result (Misra & Sudarshan 1977), in exactly this form Facchi and Pascazio's eq. 3.3 ($\gamma_{\rm eff} \sim \tau/\tau_Z^2$); anti-Zeno in a structured band is Kofman & Kurizki (2000); and the arctan formula is Kofman and Kurizki's (2001) dephasing result plus one integral over a flat band, whose strong-record limit matches Facchi and Pascazio's eqs. 4.9–4.10 and Schulman (1998). The only addition here is the reading in Barandes's terms, watching as a chain of division events (his Correspondence §3.7, eq. 57); an ISP treatment of the Zeno effects was not found in our searches.
